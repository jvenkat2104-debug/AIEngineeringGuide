# What is engineering?

Status: Draft | Independent review pending

Engineering means solving practical problems within limits: time, cost, power, materials, safety and reliability. A working calculation or prototype is a start; an engineer must also explain its assumptions and what happens outside ideal conditions.

## Three habits to develop

1. **Choose and explain.** Compare options and say why one fits the constraints. For a sensor system, ask about accuracy, battery life and cost. For a structure, ask about loads, materials and maintenance.
2. **Investigate failure.** Ask what happens when a network drops, a measurement is wrong, a component wears out or an assumption fails.
3. **Understand the connections.** Software, electronics, machines and infrastructure depend on other parts and people. Draw those connections before making changes.

## Try it

Choose a water pump, bus or food-delivery service. Write its purpose, inputs, outputs and three constraints. Draw five connected parts. Identify one failure and describe how you would notice it.

## Check your understanding

- Can you distinguish a requirement from an assumption?
- Can you explain a trade-off rather than calling one choice universally best?
- Can you identify what must be measured or tested before trusting your solution?
