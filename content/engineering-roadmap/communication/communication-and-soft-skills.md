# Communication throughout engineering

Status: Draft | Independent review pending

Communication develops through practice and feedback. Aim to be understood; elaborate vocabulary is unnecessary. Learn concepts in a language that helps you understand while practising the technical terms used in your course.

| Year | Practice | Evidence to keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Explain one concept in two minutes each week | A short explanation in your own words |
| 2 | Write instructions and present a project in five minutes | README, diagram and peer feedback |
| 3 | Write internship emails and practise team feedback | A tailored application and meeting notes |
| 4 | Rehearse interviews and explain trade-offs | A project walkthrough and improved answers |

## A useful technical explanation

Start with the problem. Name the audience and constraints. Use a diagram or concrete example, explain your decision, and finish with the result and limitations. Separate what you measured from what you assume.

## Ask for actionable feedback

After a presentation ask: Which part was unclear? What evidence was missing? Could you reproduce the result from my instructions? Choose one improvement and repeat the exercise.

## Internship email outline

Introduce yourself and your course/year. Explain why the team’s work interests you. Link one relevant project, describe what you did, and make a specific request. Keep it brief and never attach confidential work or send generic claims you cannot substantiate.

A college speaking group, peer practice session or project demonstration can provide regular practice. See the shared resources for technical writing and English learning sources.
