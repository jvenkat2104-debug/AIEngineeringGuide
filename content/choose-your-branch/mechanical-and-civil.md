# Mechanical and Civil Branches

## Mechanical Engineering
In plain language: understanding machines, motion, forces, heat and how physical products are designed and manufactured.

Subjects to look for: mechanics, materials, thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, manufacturing and design. Thermodynamics studies heat, work and energy; fluid mechanics studies liquids and gases. [1]

Everyday example: a water pump needs suitable moving parts, materials, power and a design that handles the required flow.

Try before choosing: sketch a bicycle brake or a hand-operated tool. Trace how a force moves through its parts. Propose a change and explain its effect on weight, cost or ease of use. Do not dismantle safety-critical equipment.

Possible directions to investigate: product design, manufacturing, quality, thermal systems, maintenance and automotive engineering.

Make college count: practise engineering drawing, use workshops under supervision, learn computer-aided design and test a simple design. Software and data skills can complement the physical engineering foundation.

## Civil Engineering
In plain language: designing, building and maintaining infrastructure such as buildings, roads, water systems and foundations.

Subjects to look for: structural analysis, construction materials, soil mechanics, surveying, water engineering and transport engineering. Surveying measures positions and distances; soil mechanics studies how ground behaves under loads. [1]

Everyday example: a neighbourhood drainage system must account for rainfall, ground levels, flow paths and maintenance.

Try before choosing: draw a rough map of a familiar street and mark where rainwater collects. Propose questions an engineer would investigate. This is an observation activity, not a construction design.

Possible directions to investigate: structural or site engineering, construction planning, transport, geotechnical work and water systems. Specialist responsibilities can require additional qualifications and experience.

Make college count: combine calculations with drawing, supervised field observation and material testing. Practise documenting measurements and explaining assumptions.

## Other branches deserve exploration too
Chemical, Materials, Biotechnology, Biomedical, Aerospace, Agricultural, Environmental, Instrumentation, Mechatronics and Mathematics and Computing programmes may suit different interests. This first edition profiles six areas; it does not rank them above other disciplines. Ask the same questions about subjects, practical work, prerequisites and career routes. Quantum-related titles need close checking of the actual degree, physics and maths content, and likely further-study pathway.
