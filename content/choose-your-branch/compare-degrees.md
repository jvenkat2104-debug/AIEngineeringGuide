# Compare the Actual Degree

## Broad programme or specialisation
A programme title is a starting point. Compare required subjects, optional subjects, lab hours, faculty expertise and the final degree awarded.

A broad programme can offer room to explore different areas when its curriculum supports that breadth. A specialised programme can offer focused study when it retains suitable foundations and has the faculty and facilities to teach the specialism well. Neither label alone proves quality.

| Comparison | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| CSE and CSE with AI or AI and ML | Programming, algorithms, systems, mathematics, AI depth and elective flexibility |
| CSE and IT | Required systems and software subjects, practical work and recruiter eligibility |
| ECE and an electronics specialisation | Circuit and signal foundations, lab access, design tools and elective breadth |
| EE and EEE | Balance of power, machines, electronics, controls and practical work |
| Mechanical and Mechatronics | Depth of mechanics and manufacturing versus electronics, controls and integration |

## Questions that reveal more than the name
- Can I see the semester-wise syllabus for my admission batch?
- Which subjects are compulsory and which are optional?
- Who teaches the specialist subjects, and what lab work do students complete?
- Can students take electives or a minor outside their branch? What are the actual rules?
- Which recruiters allowed students from this exact programme to apply in recent years?
- What happens if an elective is listed but too few students enrol?

## Common assumptions to question
An AI title does not guarantee an AI role. A core engineering branch does not prevent learning software, but some employers restrict eligibility by degree. A degree outside CSE does not automatically provide the same computing depth as CSE. Check the requirements of the route you want.

Do not accept an unsuitable branch on the assumption that you can change it later. Branch changes, minors, transfers and postgraduate eligibility depend on institutional rules and available places.

If engineering itself feels uncertain, compare other routes such as BSc, BCA, diploma programmes or design based on your interests and their current admission requirements. A different qualification may have a different duration and progression route.
