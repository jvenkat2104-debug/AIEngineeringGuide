# Start with Your Interests

Use these prompts to discover options. They suggest places to explore; they do not assign you a branch.

| If you enjoy exploring | Branches worth investigating |
| --- | --- |
| Logical puzzles and how apps work | CSE or IT; AI and Data Science |
| Patterns in numbers and predictions | AI and Data Science; CSE; Mathematics and Computing |
| Phones, sensors and communication devices | Electronics and Communication; Electrical |
| Electricity, motors and energy | Electrical; Electronics; Mechanical |
| Machines, movement and making things | Mechanical; Mechatronics; Electrical |
| Buildings, roads, water and the environment | Civil; Environmental programmes |

## A familiar example connects the branches
Think about a bus. Mechanical engineers can work on movement and cooling. Electrical engineers can work on motors and power. Electronics engineers can work on sensors and control devices. Software engineers can build ticketing and tracking systems. Data specialists can analyse delays. Civil engineers can work on roads and transport infrastructure.

These are illustrative connections. Real teams overlap, and each role requires relevant skills. A degree title does not automatically qualify someone for every related job.

## A seven day exploration plan
- Day 1: Write down three problems you would like to understand or improve.
- Days 2 to 4: Try one activity from each shortlisted branch. A paper exercise or simulation is enough.
- Day 5: Compare the first two years of the actual college syllabi. Circle unfamiliar subjects and look up their meanings.
- Day 6: Ask a student and a professional what they actually study or do each week.
- Day 7: Record what interested you, what frustrated you, and what you want to investigate next.

Ask yourself: Did I enjoy figuring out the problem, including the difficult parts? Would I spend another hour learning it? Lack of access or prior teaching should be recorded separately from lack of interest.
