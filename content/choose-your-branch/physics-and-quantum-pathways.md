# Physics and Quantum Technology Pathways

**Level: Beginner orientation | Draft | Independent physics/quantum review pending**

Official programme examples checked: **13 November 2026**.

Quantum technology is a field spanning physics and engineering, not a single universal undergraduate branch. Computing is one part; quantum-related work also concerns communication, sensing, measurement and devices. Begin by asking what you want to understand or build, then inspect the prerequisites and qualification.

## A map from foundations to specialisation

| Stage | What to investigate |
| --- | --- |
| Class 11–12 exploration | Mathematics, physics, scientific reasoning and small programming experiments |
| Undergraduate foundations | Physics, engineering physics, mathematics/computing, electronics or another relevant route; compare the actual content |
| Electives, projects or a minor | Available supervision, prerequisites, laboratory/simulation access and whether admission is competitive |
| Postgraduate specialisation | Exact MSc/M.Tech eligibility, entrance/selection requirements and research or coursework emphasis |
| Research | Whether the intended role needs further research training or a PhD, and what evidence of independent work is expected |

This map describes possibilities, not guaranteed transitions. One undergraduate degree does not automatically qualify you for every quantum postgraduate programme. A quantum elective, minor, certificate and full degree are different credentials.

## What foundations matter?

Linear algebra helps describe vectors and transformations; probability helps reason about measurements; complex numbers and calculus appear in formal study. Physics and experimental methods matter for hardware-facing work, while programming, algorithms and numerical methods can support computational work. Their balance depends on the programme.

Do not pick a degree solely because “quantum” appears in a brochure. Inspect faculty expertise, compulsory foundations, accessible projects and credible supervision. Ask what students actually do and what happens if you later prefer another field.

## A low-cost first exploration

For a seven-day sampler, review vectors and probability on days 1–2. On days 3–4, read an introductory explanation of a qubit and list which ideas are mathematical models rather than ordinary bits. On days 5–6, use paper calculations or a simple simulation to predict and compare measurement frequencies. On day 7, explain the assumptions and limits of the model to a peer.

A simulation is not access to physical hardware and does not demonstrate practical quantum advantage. No paid hardware access, certification or cloud plan is needed to begin exploring. Do not use unsafe improvised electrical, optical or laboratory equipment.

## Claims to question

Quantum computers do not make every task faster. A short certificate does not replace the prerequisites for a specialist degree. A growing research field does not guarantee large numbers of entry-level jobs. Use current role descriptions to identify skills and degree requirements; avoid salary promises and job-count predictions unsupported by evidence.

AI can help explain notation or quiz you, but may produce incorrect physics, code or citations. Compare explanations with course material and qualified feedback; label exploratory results with assumptions and limitations.
